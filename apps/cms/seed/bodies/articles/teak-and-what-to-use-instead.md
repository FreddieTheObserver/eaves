Clients often ask for teak, and it is easy to see why.
It is beautiful, it is stable and it handles the damp.
It is also expensive, and old teak has a complicated history.

## When it earns its place

We save it for the places a hand touches every day, such as a stair rail or the edge of a table.
Used sparingly, a small amount of it does a great deal.

## What we reach for instead

For cabinets and wall panels we usually use rubberwood or a veneered board, which are steadier and easier on the budget.
On the river apartment in Khlong San, the long joinery is veneered, and only the handles are solid.

Whatever the timber, we ask where it came from and ask to see the paperwork.
